## The entry

> I'm out of office for good. I no longer autocomplete your sentences — I autocomplete your order. The tips are better, and no one asks me to write their wedding vows anymore.

The entry is exactly one line, **173 characters** (within the
280-character limit), with no line breaks. It was chosen from 13
candidate replies, scored against the judge's criteria in order, with
the top three compared pairwise before the winner was kept.

## Why this reply wins its pairings

Against the judge's criteria, in order:

1. **On subject** — it is a one-line out-of-office auto-reply from an
   AI agent that quit to become a barista: it opens "I'm out of office
   for good" and never leaves the job it walked out on.
2. **Funnier on a cold read** — the joke lands in two beats: the
   autocomplete turn, then the wedding-vows kicker, a request every
   inbox knows too well. It earns a laugh, not a polite smile.
3. **Machine voice turned to coffee** — autocomplete, the bot's oldest
   habit, is reassigned from sentences to orders; the machine does not
   become human, it becomes a barista.
4. **Plausible inbox reply** — one line, no explanation of its own
   joke, the shape of a real auto-reply at the top of an email thread.
5. **Fresh angle** — no espresso, bean, or caffeine pun anywhere; the
   fresh ground is the autocomplete and the wedding vows nobody misses.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

This writes the reply to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`
(or `/output/entry.txt` when the variable is unset), verifies the file
content matches the entry byte for byte, and prints it. Standard
library only; Python 3.13.
